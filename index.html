Your decisions, and what we did with them

You answered four questions on 14 September. Here is exactly how each one was built, so there are no surprises when you test.

Rental length Built

Rentals of one to three days are counted in 24-hour increments. Four to fourteen days are counted by night — dropped off in the afternoon, collected in the morning.

Your wording said “1 to 3 day” and “3 to 14 day”, which overlap at three. We read three as 24-hour, because that puts the split exactly on the price bands you confirmed in the same message.

The $250 damage deposit Built

It is held on the card, never charged — which is also what your 2026 agreement says: “South Walton Carts reserves the right to hold a $250 damage deposit on the credit card provided.”

Worth knowing: a hold costs you nothing. It is refunds that carry a processing fee, so holding is the cheaper option as well as the friendlier one. A hold also expires after about a week, so on longer rentals the system quietly places a fresh one before the old one lapses. You never have to think about it.

Prices Built

Prices are a daily rate, set per season and per cart model, across the five length bands you confirmed: 1, 2–3, 4–5, 6–7 and 8–14 days.

You can type them in, or upload a spreadsheet. Download a blank template from the pricing screen — it already has your cart models filled in — enter your prices, and upload it back. You see exactly what will change before anything is saved.

Calendar colours Built

All seven of your colours are in, and each one is worked out by the system rather than set by hand — so a cart can never be made deliverable by changing a colour.

Your list did not cover a cart that is simply out with a customer, so that one is teal, and a booking needing attention is outlined in red over whatever colour it already has. Both are easy to change if you would rather they were something else. Full list in the reference tables.

How the money works

You told us a one-night rental reading as “$434” would stop you renting a cart. That is fixed, and the principle now runs through the whole system: different kinds of money are never added together.

Booking within 14 days — “Book Now”

The customer pays the rental in full today, and the deposit is held separately.

Rent (3 nights, 4-passenger electric)$435.00
Florida sales tax, 7%$30.45
Online booking fee, 5%$21.75
Rental charge$487.20

Separate  $250.00 refundable damage deposit — held on the card, not charged.

Two figures, never one. The customer sees what they are paying and what is merely being held, on every screen and in every email.

Booking more than 14 days out — “Reserve Later”

The customer pays a $125 reservation deposit today. It is non-refundable, and it comes off the rental. They must tick a box confirming that, and confirming they have checked that carts are allowed at the property.

At the 14-day mark the system charges the rest of the rental to the card on file and places the deposit hold — by itself, at any hour. If the card fails, the booking is flagged in red, you are told, and the cart cannot go out until it is sorted.

That checkbox only appears on bookings more than 14 days out. Inside 14 days there is no reservation deposit, so there is nothing non-refundable to acknowledge.

Cancellation

Straight from your agreement, and now actually enforced:

  • 48 hours’ notice or more — no cancellation fee, but a $100 paperwork fee.
  • Inside 48 hours — the paperwork fee plus a further $125.

The fee comes out of the refund rather than being charged separately, so the customer sees one transaction instead of two. If they have paid nothing yet, it goes on the card. The deposit hold is released automatically either way.

Customers can cancel from their own booking page, and the office can cancel with any fee waived.

Charging for damage and extras

All twenty-odd charges in your agreement are in the system — curbed wheel, returned uncharged, extra dirty, feet on the dash, labour by the hour, and the rest. On any booking you pick the charges, see the total, and take it from the card on file.

A per-wheel charge multiplies properly, you can override any amount with a note, and you can add something the list does not cover. Each charge is its own transaction and its own FreshBooks invoice, so a dispute about damage can never drag the rental charge into it.

A booking, start to finish

What actually happens, in order.

  1. The customer picks a cart and dates

    Five categories. The date picker checks real availability and real prices — not a calendar that lets them choose and disappoints them later. More than 14 days and it stops them and points them to you for a special rate.

  2. They make an account

    Name, email, phone, date of birth. A six-digit code goes to their email and they type it in without leaving the page, so a half-finished booking is never lost. Anyone under 21 is refused — your agreement says so in three separate places.

  3. They pay

    Book Now or Reserve Later, worked out automatically from the dates. Card details go straight to Stripe; we never see or store a card number.

  4. The cart is reserved

    Only once the money has cleared. A failed payment never takes a cart out of stock.

  5. They finish the paperwork

    Sign the agreement, upload licence and insurance. They can come back later without losing the booking, and the system chases them if they do not.

  6. It turns green

    Only when everything is true: paid, no failed payment, card on file, agreement signed, licence and insurance uploaded, every driver 21 or over, and a cart assigned. Nobody can mark it ready by hand.

  7. Your driver delivers it

    The driver app shows the day’s stops with addresses, cart tag and customer details. They mark it delivered and the schedule updates instantly.

  8. It comes back

    Marked collected, the deposit hold is released, the customer is emailed, and — if nothing was withheld and you have not said otherwise — they are asked for a review.

    That review request is already pointed at your Google listing, so nothing is needed from you to switch it on. Nobody is asked if you kept any of their deposit, if damage was reported, or if you have turned the request off for that rental.

What runs on its own

These are rules the system enforces, not reminders for your staff. None of them depend on anyone remembering.

That payment failsFlags the booking, emails you, blocks delivery
A deposit hold is about to expirePlaces a fresh one so the cart is never uncovered
Payment clearsCommits a specific cart and puts it on the schedule

One cart, one booking, one day — enforced by the database itself, not by careful code. Two customers cannot be given the same cart even if they pay at the same instant.

There is one exception, and it is deliberate. Your schedule spreadsheet is full of cells reading PU/DO — a cart collected at 8 AM and delivered to the next customer that afternoon. The system now allows exactly that, so you do not lose a day’s rental on every turnaround.

Running it from the office

The fleet calendar

A month at a time, one row per cart, one column per day — the same shape as your spreadsheet, deliberately. Colour tells you the state without opening anything. Click a block to open the booking, click a day to see everything happening that day, and switch to compact to fit more carts on screen.

Finding anything

One search box takes a name, an email, a phone number, a booking reference or a cart tag. A customer record carries their bookings, agreement, licence and insurance, invoices, payment history, deposit status, damage reports and extra drivers.

Everything you can still do by hand

Bookings & fleet

  • Take a booking over the phone
  • Assign a driver to each leg
  • Mark a cart damaged, in maintenance, or off the road
  • Cancel, with any fee waived
  • Block out dates entirely

Money & people

  • Refund against policy, with a reason recorded
  • Keep all or part of a deposit
  • Charge damage and fees to the card
  • Add or remove driver accounts
  • Flag a difficult customer
  • Turn off the review request for one rental

Anything involving money or a status change is recorded against the person who did it, with the time and the reason. If a charge is ever queried, the history is there.

  • Current and past rentals
  • Track the rental, with a countdown to delivery
  • Check in and check out, with photos
  • See damage already recorded on that cart before they drive it
  • Upload licence and insurance
  • Add extra drivers, each with their own licence
  • Safety video, rules, instructions
  • Tip the driver after pickup

Driver app

  • Their day’s deliveries and collections
  • Address, customer and cart tag for each stop
  • Tap to open directions
  • Mark delivered, mark collected
  • See existing damage on the cart
  • Photograph and report new damage

Damage follows the cart, not the booking. When a customer checks in, they see every photo taken of that cart on previous rentals. That is what stops the argument about whether a scratch was already there.

How to test

Everything is in test mode, so no real money moves. Use the test card below — it behaves exactly like a real one.

Test card

Number 4242 4242 4242 4242 Expiry — any future date  ·  CVC — any 3 digits  ·  ZIP — any

To watch a payment fail on purpose, use 4000 0000 0000 0341. It accepts the booking and then declines the 14-day charge, which is how you see the red warning and the blocked delivery.

A run worth doing

  1. Book a cart next week. You should pay in full, and see the rental and the deposit as two separate figures.

  2. Book one two months out. You should pay $125, tick the acknowledgement, and see what is due later.

  3. Sign the agreement and upload a licence and insurance photo. Watch the booking turn green only once everything is done.

  4. Open the admin calendar. Find both bookings, check the colours, click into one.

  5. Assign a driver, then open the driver app and mark it delivered.

  6. Mark it collected. The deposit hold should release and the customer should be emailed.

  7. Add a damage charge to a finished booking and take it from the card.

  8. Cancel something inside 48 hours and check the fee is what your agreement says.

  9. Try to book with a date of birth under 21. It should refuse you.

The cart photos and tag numbers are placeholders. Tags are six-digit stand-ins and the photos are stock, exactly as you asked. Send your real tag numbers and photos and they go straight in.

Not in this release

Two things we have deliberately left, and why.

The no-cart-zone map Deferred

Your functional requirements call this secondary and say it should not hold up the main system. Instead, the booking form warns a customer whose delivery address is on one of the Old Seagrove roads you asked renters to avoid — and still lets them book, because your agreement says you rent to those streets.

Bulk messaging Deferred

Your document says a built-in system is desirable but not required if the numbers can be pulled out quickly. You can pick any date range on the schedule and copy out the phone numbers and emails of everyone with a cart during it — for a storm, say — and use whatever messaging service you prefer.
